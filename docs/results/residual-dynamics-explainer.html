Method & Evaluation Guide · Latent Residual Dynamics

Turning a frozen deterministic world model into a calibrated stochastic one

A deterministic latent predictor answers “where next, on average.” We add a flow-matched residual kernel that answers “with what spread, and which way could it break” — then measure the gain against the deterministic model at every rung.

Figure 1 · Architecture

A frozen nominal, a learned residual, a small memory

The deterministic JEPA predictor Φ is frozen — its point prediction is untouched. A conditional flow Tθ maps noise to the residual (what the environment’s hidden physics actually did), whitened by fixed statistics S. A one-layer GRU carries recent residual context across the episode.

Figure 2 · Evaluation protocol

Scoring against a ground-truth conditional distribution

Most stochastic world models are judged by likelihood or downstream return. We reset the simulator to one recorded state and roll it forward many times under resampled hidden physics modes (mass, friction, slip). Those forks are the true distribution of futures — so proper scoring rules compare model samples to reality, not to a proxy.

Figure 4 · What the memory actually does

Recent-context adaptation, not a hidden-mode filter

Replicated across two nominals

The GRU’s residual-prediction benefit is real and stays positive out to 200 observed transitions — but the hidden physics mode is not linearly decodable from its state at any depth (probe accuracy sits at the majority baseline). The memory adapts to recent residual context; it does not infer which of the eight modes it is in. The paper framing follows the evidence.
